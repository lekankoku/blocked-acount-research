# MKT-01-SYN — German Blocked-Account Addressable-Market Baseline

## Executive answer and market boundary

**Parent research question: What is the addressable market for German blocked accounts?**

The evidence does **not** support one point estimate for annual German blocked-account openings. It supports a twelve-pathway addressable universe, a set of gross annual applicant/administrative-flow ceilings for some of those pathways, and a narrower partial ceiling for annual deposits. A blocked account is an accepted way of proving financial means in these pathways, but it is not established as universally mandatory in any of the twelve segments; alternative evidence can include declarations of commitment, scholarships, remuneration, parental resources, bank guarantees or sponsor support depending on pathway. fileciteturn0file6 fileciteturn0file5 The Federal Foreign Office likewise describes a blocked account as one way of proving financial support, while its student guidance recognises several alternatives. citeturn1search0turn1search9

The **strongest same-year annual volume baseline** is a **partial 2025 visa-side gross addressable ceiling of approximately 128,045 administrative episodes**:

\[
99{,}388\ \text{study/study-preparation/study-application}
+
11{,}157\ \text{language-course/school-attendance}
+
\approx17{,}500\ \text{Opportunity Cards}
=
\approx128{,}045.
\]

The Foreign Office's 2025 national-visa table reports 99,388 in the combined study category and 11,157 in the combined language-course/school-attendance category; its accompanying statistics page reports approximately 17,500 Opportunity Cards. citeturn8view0turn1search4 MKT-01-06 correctly treats these as mutually compatible **2025 visa-purpose episodes**, not observed commercial account openings. fileciteturn0file1

That **≈128,045 is not a complete German TAM and is not an estimate of actual blocked-account demand**. It is partial because several relevant pathways lack a compatible 2025 numerator, the Opportunity Card statistic is visa-side rather than a deduplicated total of visa plus initial in-country issuance, and the visa statistics contain no field recording which proof-of-funds method was actually used. fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file3 Visa data also do not cover the whole potentially relevant population: EU/EEA/Swiss citizens do not use the national-visa process, and several third-country nationalities may in specified circumstances obtain the residence title after entering Germany. citeturn5search6

The **strongest quantifiable annual deposit result** is narrower still: **≤approximately €1.412 billion of partial 2025 visa-side gross addressable deposit potential, normalised to 2026 funding rules**, covering only the inseparable BA-01+BA-02 study basket and the BA-07 Opportunity Card visa channel:

\[
€1{,}183{,}114{,}752
+
€229{,}110{,}000
=
€1{,}412{,}224{,}752.
\]

The study basket maximum applies the 2026 €11,904 full-year study balance to all 99,388 inseparable study/study-preparation/study-application cases solely to establish the basket's mathematical maximum; BA-02's nine-month maximum is lower at €9,819. The Opportunity Card ceiling applies €13,092 to approximately 17,500 visa-side cases. Current federal guidance confirms €11,904 for study, €1,091 per month for study-place search, and €1,091 per month for the Opportunity Card. citeturn2search1turn3view1 These calculations are ceilings, not forecasts of realised deposits. fileciteturn0file0

The 11,157-case BA-05+BA-08 language/school basket is included in the **account-volume ceiling** but deliberately excluded from the **€1.412 billion deposit ceiling**: BA-05 has a clear 2026 balance rule, but the combined volume does not reveal the BA-05/BA-08 composition and BA-08 does not have one defensible nationwide blocked-account amount covering school attendance and pupil exchange. Assigning one balance to all 11,157 cases would introduce an unsupported assumption. fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file0

Accordingly, the answer to MKT-01 is:

> **The current evidence establishes a twelve-pathway German blocked-account addressable universe, but not a complete annual realised-demand number. The strongest same-year measurable gross ceiling is ≤≈128,045 relevant 2025 visa-side episodes across the study, language/school and Opportunity Card categories. Within that population, actual blocked-account usage is unknown. The strongest monetised subset is ≤≈€1.412 billion of gross annual deposit potential at 2026 balance rules for the study basket plus Opportunity Card only. BA-03 adds a separate ≤19,910-case 2024 historical pathway ceiling and BA-06 a separate ≤4,240-case 2022 historical pathway ceiling; neither may be added to the 2025 figure to manufacture a mixed-vintage annual market.** fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file0

Crucially, **no evidence-backed blocked-account adoption rate, positive lower bound, or central estimate of actual annual blocked-account openings has been established**. The existence of alternative proofs of funds is evidence that gross eligible populations can overstate realised blocked-account demand; it is not evidence for a particular percentage reduction. fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file1

## Evidence state and interpretation

### Confirmed Facts

The twelve included segments form the minimum defensible blocked-account-relevant pathway universe identified by MKT-01-01. Inclusion means that livelihood/financial evidence is relevant and an authoritative source establishes blocked-account relevance for the defined pathway or conditional subset; it does **not** mean everyone in that segment opens one. fileciteturn0file6

The strongest 2025 administrative flow evidence consists of 99,388 cases in the combined study category, 11,157 in language-course/school attendance and approximately 17,500 Opportunity Cards. citeturn8view0turn1search4 The same Foreign Office table reports 214,034 employment-category cases, but the category includes scientists, highly qualified people, internships, au pairs, voluntary services and other purposes; it is therefore not a defensible numerator for BA-09, BA-11 or BA-12. citeturn8view0 fileciteturn0file3

Current 2026 federal funding rules establish, among other values, €11,904 for a full year of study; €1,091 per month for study-place search and the Opportunity Card; €959 per month for school-based vocational training; and €1,091 per month for relevant recognition pathways. citeturn2search1turn3view0turn3view1turn3view2 The Residence Act's livelihood framework itself differentiates the benchmark families applicable to education/search purposes. citeturn1search12

**Directly established blocked-account usage is different from blocked-account applicability.** Applicability is directly established throughout the taxonomy at the required level of specificity; **actual use is not**. None of the completed annual-volume datasets contains an observed financing-method field that identifies which applicants actually opened or used a blocked account. fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file1

### Evidence-backed Estimates

The **≈128,045** partial 2025 visa-side gross ceiling is a transparent arithmetic aggregation of compatible official visa-purpose categories, not an adoption estimate. fileciteturn0file1

The **≤€1.412224752 billion** deposit figure is likewise an arithmetic upper ceiling under explicitly documented 2026-rule normalisation. It is not a prediction of deposits that providers will receive. fileciteturn0file0

The BA-03 **≤€229.124 million** and BA-06 **≤€55.510 million** values are historical-proxy upper envelopes, each preserved in its original volume vintage rather than being summed into the 2025 result. fileciteturn0file0

### Assumptions

Only narrow, disclosed calculation conventions are used. The account-ceiling presentation treats an eligible administrative/person-purpose episode as **at most one potential new-account opportunity**. This is a market unit, not an assertion that every episode generates a new commercial account. fileciteturn0file2

The monetised ceilings use current 2026 balance rules against the stated historical annual-flow baselines. They therefore answer, “what maximum funding requirement would this observed annual flow imply under the current rule?”, not “how much money was actually deposited in that historical year”. fileciteturn0file0

For BA-01+BA-02, BA-03 and BA-06, the reported euro ceilings intentionally use **balance-maximising compositions**. They are boundary conditions rather than expected case mixes. fileciteturn0file0

No assumption is made for blocked-account adoption, growth, account reuse, segment allocation inside combined official categories, or shares of broad employment categories. fileciteturn0file2

### Hypotheses

MKT-01-01 records possible additional blocked-account relevance in routes such as some self-employment/freelance or exceptional financing situations outside the twelve-segment minimum universe, but the evidence was insufficient to establish a nationwide countable segment. These remain **upside/watchlist hypotheses and are excluded from the market baseline**. fileciteturn0file6

### Unknowns

The largest unresolved variable is **actual blocked-account adoption/use by pathway**. No positive empirical lower bound and no central estimate of account openings is supported by the completed research. fileciteturn0file1

Other material unknowns include the BA-01/BA-02 split; BA-05/BA-08 split; BA-03 school/company composition and remuneration shortfalls; a current BA-06 annual flow and financing mix; complete BA-07 initial issuance including in-country cases; isolated annual volumes for BA-04 and BA-09 through BA-12; the annual incidence of reused versus newly opened accounts; and the current nationwide BA-12 shortfall amount. fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file1

These are **evidence gaps**, not missing arithmetic. Filling them would require new administrative data, provider/account-opening data or empirical financing-method evidence rather than a modelling assumption.

### Contradictory Evidence

For BA-06, current central federal recognition guidance supports **€1,091 per month**, whereas the German Embassy Bern checklist dated 20 March 2026 states **€992 per month**. The synthesis follows MKT-01-05 in using the central rule for a national baseline while retaining the Bern figure as contradictory jurisdictional evidence; no average is taken. fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file2 citeturn3view2

For BA-12, Bern states **€855** while the individual components shown in its evidence sum to **€812**; a separate Dar es Salaam figure of €855 is explicitly a 2025 figure. MKT-01-02 therefore correctly leaves a nationwide current 2026 numeric balance unknown. fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file2

The Foreign Office's visa publication also uses potentially confusing outcome terminology: its overall statistics page discusses processed visa applications while referring to the annual national-visa total using issuance language, whereas the detailed table is headed “Bearbeitete nationale Visa 2025”. The synthesis therefore preserves these figures conservatively as **national-visa administrative cases** and does not claim they are unique applicants, approvals, arrivals or blocked-account openings. fileciteturn0file2 citeturn1search4turn8view0

## Downstream handoff

### MKT-02 — Build TAM / SAM / SOM model

MKT-02 should ingest the market baseline as an **evidence-constrained input layer**, rather than convert every eligible episode into a customer.

The correct gross-volume base is the annual **person-purpose / relevant administrative episode**, with combined official categories preserved where they cannot be split. The 2025 99,388 study basket must therefore remain BA-01+BA-02, and the 11,157 language/school basket must remain BA-05+BA-08 unless a compatible official split is subsequently obtained. fileciteturn0file2 citeturn8view0

For an account TAM, the model should conceptually distinguish:

\[
\text{gross relevant episodes}
\rightarrow
\text{own-funding / blocked-account-relevant subset}
\rightarrow
\text{actual blocked-account users}
\rightarrow
\text{new commercial accounts}.
\]

Only the first stage is quantified for the principal 2025 baskets. The later stages are presently **unknown** and should remain nullable/modelled parameters rather than receiving arbitrary defaults. The fact that a declaration of commitment, scholarship, remuneration or another financing method exists does not identify the frequency with which applicants use it. fileciteturn0file5 fileciteturn0file1

MKT-02 should keep the **2025 partial ceiling of ≤≈128,045 episodes** separate from BA-03's 2024 ≤19,910 and BA-06's 2022 ≤4,240 proxies until comparable same-year flows exist. Summing the three vintages would create a synthetic annual market not observed in any year. fileciteturn0file1

For monetary modelling, **€1.412bn is a gross annual new-deposit ceiling, not AUM**. A deposit received during the year, the amount remaining blocked at a point in time, and average provider assets under administration are different metrics because funds are released over time and account duration varies. MKT-01-07 supplies only the first concept. fileciteturn0file0

The handoff variables should therefore include at minimum: segment/basket identifier; data year; administrative stage; gross annual episode ceiling; balance rule and effective year; conditional maximum deposit; observed blocked-account-use rate (**null**); new-account/reuse conversion (**null**); and explicit compatibility flags identifying which rows may be summed. fileciteturn0file2

A commercially useful SAM or SOM may subsequently apply provider-country eligibility, product constraints, acquisition reach, price, channel or competition factors, but those are **new model inputs** and should not be retrofitted into this research baseline.

### MKT-03 — Rank target source countries

MKT-03 should rank countries using **annual relevant episode flows**, not international-student stocks. The Foreign Office 2025 national-visa table is well suited to a visa-channel source-country ranking because it is reported by mission/country and preserves purpose categories, including the 99,388 study basket and 11,157 language/school basket. citeturn8view0 MKT-01-03 establishes why the approximately 402,000 international-student installed population is not an annual account-acquisition denominator. fileciteturn0file4

The same aggregation rules must survive the country ranking. MKT-03 should not allocate national or mission-level study cases between BA-01 and BA-02, nor language/school cases between BA-05 and BA-08, unless the source itself provides a compatible split. fileciteturn0file2

A **visa-source-country ranking is not automatically the same as total German addressable-demand ranking**. EU/EEA/Swiss citizens are outside the national-visa process, and nationals of Australia, Canada, Israel, Japan, New Zealand, South Korea, the United Kingdom and the United States can in specified circumstances enter without a national visa and obtain the relevant residence title in Germany. citeturn5search6 MKT-03 should therefore label a ranking based on Foreign Office statistics explicitly as **visa-side gross relevant episodes**, rather than “all German blocked-account demand by country”.

Country-level blocked-account demand should also **not** be inferred by applying a common adoption percentage to visa volumes. Until country-level financing-method or provider-opening evidence exists, the defensible output is a ranking of **gross relevant source-country episode ceilings**, with actual blocked-account propensity separately marked unknown. fileciteturn0file1
